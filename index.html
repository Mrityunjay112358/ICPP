Our Story

A movement, born quietly.

What began as field exposure during Ariana's early public service internship became a long-term commitment to protecting children from exploitation.

Working across vulnerable communities in India and beyond, she built a model that goes beyond rescue — connecting children to recovery, education, and reintegration.

Today, the work spans rescue interventions, prevention systems, and pathways that help children reclaim safe futures.

How We Work

Two parallel pathways.

We support children already affected by exploitation, and prevent vulnerable children from entering those systems in the first place.

Response

Rescue → Recovery → Reintegration

Supporting children already affected by exploitation, from rescue to a safe future.

  • Rescue

    Identifying vulnerable children through field research, local networks, and government coordination.

  • Recovery

    Providing trauma-informed counselling, emotional support, and bridge learning spaces.

  • Reintegration

    Transitioning children into formal education through partnerships with schools and grassroots institutions.

Prevention

Prevention → Protection → System Change

Stopping vulnerable children from entering exploitation systems in the first place.

  • Prevention

    Early identification of at-risk children in informal settlements before exploitation occurs.

  • Protection

    Volunteer-led education, legal literacy, and welfare linkages for vulnerable families.

  • System Change

    Research, policy advocacy, and scalable partnerships that address structural causes of exploitation.

This model ensures that children are never pushed into exploitation and out of school.

Global Success

From Rajasthan, to the world.

What began in Rajasthan has grown into a movement across countries.

Note: we haven't disclosed or uploaded any pictures of the rescued girls we work with to preserve their safety.

India.

Project Rang began in India, working within informal worker communities where children are often pushed out of school due to financial pressure. Here, the foundation of the model was built.

i.

Bridge learning programmes across 15 districts

Operating last-mile bridge schools that bring out-of-school children back to learning environments across rural and informal-settlement communities.

ii.

Strengthened 550+ rural schools

Partnered with Students For Last-Mile Learning to reinforce school infrastructure, teacher support, and learning resources in remote schools.

iii.

5,000+ households linked to welfare schemes

Connecting vulnerable families to government welfare benefits — the financial foundation that lets children stay in school.

iv.

Partnerships with Ekal Vidyalaya & Friends of Tribal Society

Working alongside India's largest single-teacher school network to extend reintegration pathways into tribal and rural belts.

v.

Asia Record for the largest legal rights awareness campaign

A record-breaking literacy drive equipping women and families in rural India with the legal awareness needed to protect their rights.

Nepal.

In Nepal, the barrier is not just access, but disruption. Children affected by conflict and displacement face interrupted education, instability, and emotional stress that directly impact their ability to learn. Here, the foundation of the model was built.

i.

Bridge learning across Kathmandu

Restoring continuity of education for displaced children through community-led bridge classrooms.

ii.

1,500+ households linked to welfare schemes

Connecting families to government and NGO welfare systems so financial pressure doesn't force children out of school.

iii.

Mental health & resilience sessions

Counselling, group resilience workshops, and trauma-informed care for children growing up amid displacement.
